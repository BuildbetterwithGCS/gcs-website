Why Spatial

A Table Cannot Show You Adjacency

A condition register tells you that Lift Station 4 is in poor condition with eleven open work orders. A capital plan tells you that Ridge Road culvert replacement is bid over estimate. A risk register tells you a flood corridor activated last October.

None of those documents tells you that all three are within a half mile of each other, that the culvert project's detour route runs through the flood corridor, and that the lift station loses service when that corridor floods. That relationship only becomes visible when the operating record is placed on the ground.

This is the practical argument for spatial intelligence in operations, and it has nothing to do with maps being attractive. Adjacency drives sequencing. Two projects on the same corridor should be coordinated; two failures behind the same single point of failure should be treated as one problem; a risk zone that overlaps three critical assets is not the same risk as one that overlaps none.

Map Intelligence is a view of the same operating record that drives every other Nexus module — not a separate system with its own data. An asset's condition changes once, and the map, the dashboard, and the capital model all reflect it.

Built Without

No external map service

This demonstration renders entirely in the browser with SVG and CSS. There is no third-party tile provider, no map API key, and no request leaving your browser to any mapping service while you use this page.

That choice is deliberate for a demonstration. In a client deployment, spatial data is rendered against the client's authoritative geographic base — typically their existing GIS — under their data governance terms, rather than pushed to a public service.

  • No external tiles, keys, or trackers on this page
  • Layer state and selection are local to your browser
  • Synthetic geography — no real parcels or utilities
Capabilities

What Map Intelligence Provides

Six spatial capabilities that change how operational and capital decisions get made.

Layered operating picture

Assets, projects, facilities, risk zones, and network routes drawn as independent layers that can be combined to answer a specific question rather than crowded onto one static map.

Exposure analysis

See which assets fall inside a flood corridor, slope watch area, or service interruption zone — and how many of those are critical with no redundancy.

Corridor coordination

Identify capital projects, utility work, and maintenance activity competing for the same corridor before they are scheduled into conflict.

Condition at a glance

Condition and criticality expressed spatially, so degradation clusters become visible — which is usually where a systemic cause is hiding.

Capital sequencing

Evaluate programmed work geographically to find adjacency savings, avoid tearing up new pavement, and sequence around access constraints.

Response readiness

During an event, see which assets are in the affected area, what condition they were in going in, and which access routes are compromised.

Honest Scope

What This Demonstration Is and Is Not

Represents accurately

The interaction model

  • Layer control and visibility management
  • Condition and category filtering with context preserved
  • Record selection returning full operational detail
  • Network routes and zones rendered as distinct layers
  • Status expressed consistently with the rest of Nexus
Simplified here

The geography

  • Synthetic coordinates, not a projected coordinate system
  • A dozen records rather than thousands
  • Illustrative routes rather than surveyed network geometry
  • No basemap, imagery, parcel fabric, or elevation model
  • No measurement, routing, or spatial query tools
In a deployment

The real implementation

  • Rendered against the client's authoritative GIS base
  • Bidirectional link between spatial features and asset records
  • Condition, work order, and capital status driven by live data
  • Access controls consistent with the client's data governance
  • Export to the formats an engineering department already uses

On GIS. Most organizations that need this already have GIS, and often have had it for years. The problem is rarely the absence of spatial data — it is that the spatial layer and the operating record are separate systems that disagree. Map Intelligence is designed to join them, not to replace a working GIS investment.
